A Very Important Message

Before Rebuilding the ESP — Check the Electrical System

An ESP's performance is influenced not only by its mechanical configuration but also by the quality and control of the electrical energy delivered to the treatment fields.

In suitable installations, modernization of the power supply and control system may provide a practical route to improving ESP electrical energization without immediately undertaking major mechanical modifications.

ESP Control Retrofit

The cheapest intervention is often the control system.

Ageing control panels can limit the operating performance of otherwise mechanically sound ESPs. Where the transformer rectifier is healthy, replacing the controller alone can change what the field is able to hold — at a fraction of the cost and downtime of a set replacement.

Three-Phase ESP Power Supply

Three-Phase Power Upgrade

Three-phase power-supply arrangements can be considered for ESP fields where the existing electrical configuration or operating performance makes such an upgrade technically appropriate.

Because all three phases are controlled and rectified, the field sees a smoother waveform and a symmetrical load is presented to the incoming supply — which matters on sites where a bank of single-phase sets has produced persistent phase imbalance.

The suitability and expected benefit of a three-phase upgrade must be established through an electrical and ESP performance assessment. Three-phase energization is not automatically superior for every ESP.

High-Frequency Power for Existing ESPs

The waveform is the whole argument.

Conventional ESP power supplies operate at mains frequency, whereas high-frequency power-supply technologies can deliver a different electrical waveform with reduced ripple and more responsive control. Because peak voltage — not average — sets the sparking threshold, ripple is what limits the average voltage a field can hold.

The Central Question

Can We Improve ESP Performance Without Major Mechanical Modification?

In suitable cases — yes.

Where the existing ESP mechanical structure, electrode geometry, gas distribution and rapping system remain fundamentally serviceable, electrical modernization can be evaluated before considering major mechanical reconstruction.

Depending on the condition of the existing system, solutions may include modern controls, three-phase power conversion, high-frequency power supplies or a combination of electrical and targeted mechanical improvements.

Every ESP is different. Aeon recommends a technical assessment before determining whether electrical retrofit alone is sufficient.

HF Power Supply Retrofit

Where improved energization is the objective.

High-frequency systems can be considered where improved electrical energization is required and the existing mechanical ESP remains suitable.

Because switching occurs far above mains frequency, spark response is measured in microseconds rather than milliseconds — which is what makes intermittent and pulsed energization genuinely controllable on high-resistivity dust.

Benefits below are stated as potential outcomes, subject to site conditions. No guaranteed emission reduction percentage is offered — the achievable result depends on the existing ESP and the process it serves.

Potential Benefits

Reduced ripple More responsive voltage / current control Better handling of sparking conditions Potential improvement in field power utilization Potential reduction in particulate emissions Potential energy optimization

When Electrical Retrofit Is Not Enough

Electrical modernization does not solve everything.

Mechanical intervention may be required where inspection identifies conditions that no amount of energization will compensate for. A field with distorted plates or a rapping system that is not releasing the dust layer will not be rescued by a better controller.

Damaged collecting electrodes Deformed discharge electrodes Poor electrode alignment Failed rapping systems Damaged insulators Structural deterioration Gas distribution problems Hopper / dust evacuation problems Excessive air ingress Severe internal wear

Aeon's approach is to identify the minimum intervention necessary to achieve the required performance — rather than automatically recommending a complete ESP rebuild.
