High-Voltage Insulation Solutions for Electrostatic Precipitators

High-voltage insulators are critical components of an Electrostatic Precipitator, providing electrical isolation between high-voltage electrode systems and grounded structures while also supporting or transmitting mechanical loads where required.

What Is an ESP HV Insulator?

The Role of HV Insulators in an ESP

HV insulators perform two primary functions, and in most ESP arrangements they perform both at once.

Electrical Isolation

They electrically isolate high-voltage components from the grounded ESP casing and supporting structures, holding the potential difference that the electrical field depends on.

Mechanical Support

Depending on the design, certain insulators also support electrode systems or transmit mechanical movement while maintaining electrical isolation.

Because the insulator carries both duties, a cracked, contaminated or incorrectly fitted unit is not only an electrical problem — it can also be a mechanical one.

Product 01

Hollow Ceramic Support Insulator

High-Voltage Support & Isolation for ESP Electrode Systems

The hollow ceramic support insulator is used as a high-voltage insulating and sealing component associated with the discharge-electrode suspension arrangement where it enters the electrical field. The suspension rod passes through the central bore, so the insulator holds the electrical break at the same point where the mechanical load is carried.

Depending on the ESP configuration, the insulator can also provide mechanical support to the discharge-electrode system, and its interface area forms part of the seal between the gas space and the insulator housing.

The glazed external surface matters as much as the body: surface condition governs how readily dust and moisture establish a leakage path across the insulator.

Product 02

ESP Post Insulators

Rigid High-Voltage Support & Electrical Isolation

Post insulators provide high-voltage insulation while mechanically supporting the associated ESP high-voltage assembly. The construction is a ceramic body with metal fittings at each end — one interfacing with the HV assembly, the other with the grounded support structure.

Unlike the hollow insulator, there is no central bore carrying a rod: the post carries the load through its own body in compression, so the mounting arrangement at both fittings determines how the load is distributed.

Where the arrangement is exposed to dust deposition, the shed profile and creepage path along the body are selected against the location rather than assumed.

Ceramic grade, binder system and fitting metallurgy are selected according to application and customer/OEM requirements.

Product 03 — ESP-Specific

ESP Shaft Insulators

Mechanical Power Transmission with High-Voltage Isolation

The shaft insulator is installed between the rapping shaft located inside the electrical field and the external drive / gear-motor arrangement. Its purpose is to allow mechanical rotation and power transmission while maintaining the required electrical insulation between the high-voltage discharge-electrode system and the grounded ESP casing.

It is the one insulator in the ESP that has to do two things which normally conflict: pass torque continuously, and block a high potential difference across the same short length. That makes both the ceramic body and the coupling interfaces at each end critical — a loose or worn coupling shows up as shaft misalignment and, eventually, as a cracked body.

Mechanical power transmission Electrical isolation boundary

Torque passes continuously from the drive to the rapping mechanism while the ceramic body holds the potential difference across the casing wall. Torque, speed and voltage values are as per ESP design and customer/OEM specification.

Classification by Type & Usage

Four insulating duties inside one precipitator.

ESP insulators serve a dual function: providing mechanical structural support within the precipitation chamber while electrically isolating the high-voltage DC electrode circuit from the grounded metal outer casing. They do it under harsh conditions — immense mechanical loads, volatile temperature swings and corrosive, dust-laden gas.

TYPE 01

Support Insulators — Suspension / Post

Rigidly hold and suspend the weight of the discharge electrode framework while keeping it isolated from the grounded casing. Typically installed atop the ESP roof inside penthouse enclosures, with compartment heaters to prevent conductive moisture or ash condensation.

TYPE 02

Shaft Insulators — Rapping / Drive

Transmit rotational mechanical force from external motors to the internal rapping system, connecting the external drive to the tumbling hammer shafts and enduring continuous torsional stress and vibration as hammers strike the plates.

TYPE 03

Wall Bushings — Feed-Through

Guide high-voltage DC bus-bars or power cables through the grounded outer walls of the ESP penthouse or transformer-rectifier enclosure.

TYPE 04

Damping Resistance Insulators

Integrated within the high-voltage secondary circuit to isolate and absorb electrical harmonic peaks and surge anomalies generated by the TR set.

Types 01 and 02 are covered as products above. Types 03 and 04 are shown for completeness of the ESP insulation picture — availability is subject to enquiry.

Form Factors

Shape follows creepage distance and load path.

Form factor design prioritises maximising the electrical creepage distance — the shortest path along the insulator surface — to prevent flashover, while maintaining the structural load threshold.

Form Factor Mechanical Profile Primary Application Engineering Focus
Conical — tapered hollow cone Wide base tapering toward the top opening. Heavy load support Distributes compressive downward force evenly across a wide base area.
Cylindrical — hollow barrel Straight, uniform vertical walls. Standard suspension Optimises internal clearances; used for suspension rod or post configurations.
Rapping pin / shaft rod Solid or thick-walled hollow rod with cast metal hardware fitted at both ends. Rapping drives & rotational shafts Maximises torsional and shear strength to survive repeated impact and rotation.

Nearly all form factors feature exterior sheds or skirts — the corrugations that break direct tracking paths along the surface.

Material Classification

Ceramic bodies, classified by duty.

Reference classification for ESP insulating bodies. Material grade and specification for any given supply are selected according to application and customer/OEM requirements.

C-130

High-Alumina Porcelain

Up to 90–95% aluminium oxide. The general standard where high mechanical strength and thermal shock resistance are both required.

C-610

Alumina-Silicate / Mullite

For stable high-temperature operation under standard continuous mechanical load.

C-795 / ULTRA-HIGH ALUMINA

Above 95% Al₂O₃

For heavy metallurgical or waste-incineration duty where temperatures exceed 400 °C, and against sudden boiler start-up thermal shock.

LOW-LOSS (LD)

Low-Dielectric-Loss Ceramic

Formulated for high-voltage DC profiles, to limit leakage current under dirty operating conditions.

SURFACE

Glaze Treatment

Finished with a high-temperature brown or white silicate glaze, giving the smooth, dust-repellent surface that keeps the creepage path clean.

Material groups per IEC 60672 classification.
