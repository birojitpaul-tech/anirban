ESP Collecting Electrodes

Precision-Engineered Collecting Electrodes for Electrostatic Precipitators

Collecting electrodes are the primary collecting surfaces inside a precipitator. Electrically charged particulate matter migrates towards the collecting electrodes under the influence of the electric field, and the accumulated dust is periodically removed through the rapping system.

Function

What the collecting electrode does.

The collecting electrode is the earthed surface the dust ends up on. Its profile, flatness and spacing decide how much of the charged particulate is captured, and how cleanly the dust layer releases when the plate is rapped.

Charging. Discharge electrodes energised from the transformer rectifier set ionise the gas around them. Ions attach to passing dust particles.

Migration. The field between the discharge electrodes and the earthed collecting electrodes drives the charged particles sideways, out of the gas stream.

Collection. Particles deposit on the collecting electrode as a dust layer. Profile geometry shields the deposited dust from the gas stream and resists re-entrainment.

Release. Rapping transmits shock through the plate so the dust layer sheds into the hopper. Plate rigidity and straightness govern how evenly that shock travels.

Collecting Electrode Profiles

Collecting electrodes are manufactured in different roll-formed profiles depending on the precipitator design, electrode spacing, mechanical requirements and OEM specifications. The profile determines the collecting area presented to the gas stream, the stiffness of the plate over its free length, and how the dust layer behaves when the plate is rapped.
