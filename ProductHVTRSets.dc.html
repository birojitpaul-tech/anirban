HV TR Sets & Controls

The transformer rectifier set and its controller together decide how much voltage an ESP field can actually hold. Aeon Tech Solutions supplies both halves — single phase, three phase and high frequency TR sets, and the control cabinets and controllers that energise them.

Two Sub-Categories

The set, and the intelligence driving it.

An ESP field is energised by a high-voltage DC supply. The TR set produces it; the controller decides — many times a second — how hard to push before the gap flashes over. Replace one without considering the other and the gain is usually smaller than expected.

Technical Detail

Three energisation technologies, three different arguments.

The choice between single phase, three phase and high frequency energisation is not a question of which is newest. It follows the field, the dust resistivity, the incoming supply and what the plant is trying to achieve.

01 — SINGLE PHASE

Conventional Thyristor-Controlled TR Sets

A single phase thyristor AC controller feeds the primary of a step-up transformer; the secondary output is rectified by a high-voltage bridge and delivered to the field through the HT bushing. The whole assembly sits in an oil-filled tank that serves as both insulating medium and cooling path, with a conservator to accommodate thermal expansion.

Because conduction is controlled by phase angle on a 50 Hz supply, the DC delivered to the field carries substantial ripple. Peak voltage — not average — sets the sparking threshold, so ripple directly limits the average voltage the field can hold.

This is the arrangement the majority of installed ESPs were built around, and it remains entirely serviceable. Where the tank, bushing and windings are sound, the sensible intervention is usually the controller rather than the set.

Characteristics

Oil-filled tank, conservator and radiators Phase-angle control at supply frequency High ripple content in the DC output Single phase load on the incoming supply Spark recovery limited by the mains half-cycle Widest installed base — spares well understood

Where it fits

Like-for-like replacement of a failed set, or new supply where the field arrangement and control philosophy are already fixed.

02 — THREE PHASE

Three Phase TR Sets

All three phases of the incoming supply are controlled and rectified, so the field sees six pulses per cycle instead of two. The DC waveform is markedly smoother, which raises the average voltage achievable before the peak reaches the sparking threshold — more corona power into the same field for the same spark rate.

The load presented to the incoming supply is symmetrical, which matters on sites where a bank of single phase sets has created persistent phase imbalance.

The set remains an oil-filled transformer, so the mechanical and maintenance picture is familiar — it is the electrical behaviour that changes, not the maintenance regime.

Characteristics

Six-pulse rectification, low ripple Higher average field voltage at the same spark rate Balanced load across all three phases Better utilisation of installed field area Conventional oil-filled construction retained Suits high-resistivity dust duties

Where it fits

Larger fields, sites with unbalanced incoming supply, and cases where a higher average field voltage is the stated objective.

03 — HIGH FREQUENCY

High Frequency Switch Mode Power Supplies

The incoming supply is rectified to a DC link, then inverted at kilohertz frequencies through a compact high-frequency transformer and rectifier. Because the switching frequency is orders of magnitude above 50 Hz, the output is near-constant DC with very little ripple, and the transformer itself is a fraction of the size and weight of an equivalent oil-filled set.

Two consequences matter operationally. Almost the full peak voltage becomes useful average voltage. And spark response is measured in microseconds rather than milliseconds — the supply can quench and re-establish the field between mains half-cycles, so a spark costs far less collection time.

The same speed makes intermittent and pulsed energisation genuinely controllable, which is what makes high frequency effective on high-resistivity dust where back-corona limits conventional sets. Units are typically dry, sealed and roof-mountable, removing oil handling from the maintenance picture.

Characteristics

Switch mode operation at kilohertz frequencies Near-ripple-free DC — peak close to average Microsecond spark detection and recovery Fine control of intermittent and pulsed energisation Compact, dry and substantially lighter — oil-free Integrated control; no separate cabinet controller

Where it fits

Emission-limit-driven upgrades, high-resistivity dust and back-corona problems, weight-constrained roofs, and retrofits where removing oil from the roof is itself an objective.

HT Switches

High-tension switches — isolation, earthing and safe access.

An HT switch sits between the TR set output and the ESP field. It performs two duties: selecting or isolating a field for maintenance, and positively earthing the high-voltage system before anyone enters the precipitator. On an ESP that second duty is the one that matters most — the field retains charge after the set is switched off.

Switching schemes
FUNCTION 01

Field Isolation

Disconnects an individual field from its TR set so work can proceed on one field while the remaining fields stay in service.

FUNCTION 02

Positive Earthing

Connects the HV system solidly to earth and holds it there, discharging residual charge in the field before internal access.

FUNCTION 03

Mechanical Interlocking

Key or castell interlocks make the earthed position a precondition of access, so the sequence cannot be defeated by procedure alone.

CONSTRUCTION

Enclosure & Insulation

Sheet steel enclosure with insulated support of the HV path, viewing window on the switch position, and cable or busbar entry to suit the installation.

SUPPLY SCOPE

Switches & Spares

Complete HT switch units, and spares for existing switches — contacts, insulators, operating mechanism, interlock hardware and position indication.

Spare Support & Repair Services

Replace the part that has failed, not the set that hasn't.

A TR set is rarely beyond use as a whole. In most cases the tank, windings and core are sound and the fault sits in a bushing, a rectifier stack, a protection device or a feedback card. Identifying which is the difference between a component replacement in a shutdown window and a capital replacement.

Spare support for existing TR set & control panel installations

Supplied individually or as a scope defined against an inspection. Ratings are confirmed against the existing set.

01

HT Bushing (36 kV / 52 kV)

Carries the rectified high-voltage output from inside the oil-filled tank to the field connection. Tracking, cracking or oil seepage at the bushing is one of the most common reasons a serviceable set is taken out of use.

02

Rectifier Stack

The high-voltage silicon diode assembly that converts the transformer secondary output to DC. A partially failed stack shows as reduced secondary voltage and a distorted V–I characteristic rather than an outright trip.

03

Pressure Switch

Protection device monitoring tank pressure and responding to the rapid rise produced by an internal fault. Supplied as a direct replacement where the existing device has failed or become unreliable.

04

Dial Thermometer

Oil temperature indication with alarm and trip contacts. Loss of reliable temperature indication removes the earliest warning of a set being worked beyond its thermal capability.

05

Voltage Feedback Card

Conditions the secondary voltage signal from the set for the controller. A drifting card causes the controller to regulate against a false reading — the field appears healthy while running below its capability.

06

Current Feedback Card

Conditions the secondary current signal for the controller and its spark detection logic. Faults here typically present as erratic spark counting or premature current limiting.

07

LT Bushing

Carries the transformer primary connections through the tank wall. Replaced where the porcelain is damaged or the seal has begun to weep at the flange.

08

Thyristor

The power semiconductor in the primary AC controller. Supplied to match the existing stack rating, with the gate lead and heatsink arrangement of the panel it replaces.

09

Firing Card

Generates the gate pulses that set the thyristor conduction angle. Degraded firing shows as unstable primary current and an inability to hold the set at its operating point.

10

Pressure Release Valve

Relieves tank pressure on an internal fault and reseats afterwards. Supplied as a direct replacement where the existing valve has seized, corroded or begun to weep oil.
