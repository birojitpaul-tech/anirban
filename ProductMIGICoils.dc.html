MIGI Rapper Coils

Electromagnetic Rapper Coils for Reliable ESP Dust Removal

MIGI (Magnetic Impulse Gravity Impact) rappers are widely used in Electrostatic Precipitators to remove accumulated dust from collecting and/or discharge electrode assemblies. The electromagnetic coil generates the lifting impulse that raises the rapper plunger, after which gravity returns the plunger to produce the mechanical impact required for dust dislodging.

Aeon Tech Solutions supplies MIGI rapper coils and associated components for new installations, replacement requirements, maintenance and ESP retrofit applications.

What Is a MIGI Rapper?

Rapping energy delivered from outside the gas stream.

MIGI stands for Magnetic Impulse Gravity Impact — the two forces the rapper uses in sequence.

A Magnetic Impulse Gravity Impact rapper uses an electromagnetic impulse to lift a plunger and gravity to return it, producing a mechanical impact that is transmitted to the relevant ESP electrode or frame structure to dislodge accumulated dust. The coil sits on the ESP roof; only the impact member reaches into the casing.

Because of that arrangement there is no rotating shaft, bearing or hammer inside the hot, dust-laden gas — the components most exposed to wear in a tumbling hammer arrangement.

Rapping intensity follows the energy in the pulse and the frequency follows the rapper control panel, so each row can be tuned independently — inlet fields, where dust load is heaviest, differently from outlet fields, where over-rapping causes re-entrainment.

02 — The Coil

ESP MIGI Rapper Coils

The coil is the electromagnetic element responsible for generating the lifting impulse in a MIGI rapper. Everything else in the assembly either guides the plunger or transmits the blow — the coil is what turns a control pulse into mechanical work.

Coil Construction

Precision-Wound Electromagnetic Coil

Reliable coil operation depends on controlled winding, suitable electrical insulation, thermal behaviour and protection from the operating environment. A rapper coil is energised repeatedly, all day, for years — so consistency of winding and the integrity of the insulation system decide service life more than any single rating does.

Copper winding and insulation system can be selected according to the required electrical and environmental conditions.

Rapping Duty

Collecting and discharge electrodes are rapped differently.

Both duties use the same operating principle, but the load they strike and the consequence of getting the intensity wrong are not the same. This is why rapper rows are grouped and controlled separately.

DUTY A

Collecting Electrode Rapping

The impact is transmitted to the collecting plate assembly to release the accumulated dust layer as agglomerated sheets that fall into the hopper.

Too little energy leaves the layer in place and reduces field strength. Too much re-entrains the dust into the gas stream — which shows at the stack.

DUTY B

Discharge Electrode Rapping

The impact is transmitted through the high-voltage frame to keep the emitting surfaces clean, so corona generation is not suppressed by dust build-up on the electrodes themselves.

The rapper must act on a live HV assembly, so the insulating element in the load path is part of the arrangement, not an accessory.

DUTY C

Gas Distribution Screen Rapping

Keeps the inlet and outlet distribution screens clear so gas velocity stays even across the full ESP cross-section.

Blinded screens distort flow distribution — a common and frequently missed cause of underperformance.

Control Chain

The coil is one link in the rapping chain.

04 — Rapper Controls

The panel decides how well the rappers work.

A MIGI installation is only as good as its rapping programme. The control panel fires each coil in a defined sequence, at a set intensity and interval, and a programme left at commissioning settings for a decade rarely still matches the dust the plant is making.

Under-rapping leaves an insulating dust layer that suppresses field strength. Over-rapping re-entrains dust that has already been collected — and shows up as a stack reading that worsens immediately after each rap.

Rapper control panels

New panels and replacements for obsolete units, with programmable sequence, intensity and interval per rapper or per group.

Sequence and intensity setting

Rapping programmes set field by field — heavier and more frequent at the inlet, lighter at the outlet to avoid re-entrainment.

Fault indication

Open-circuit and failed-coil indication, so a dead rapper is found from the panel rather than from a rising emission trend.

Integration with ESP controls

Interlocks and signalling to the ESP controller and plant DCS, including power-off rapping at shutdown.

Quality & Testing

Checked before it goes to the roof.

A rapper coil is buried in a row of identical units on an ESP roof. Finding a weak one after installation costs a shutdown, so the checks belong before despatch.

Test scope and documentation are agreed against the order. Where a customer or OEM specification defines the test regime, that specification governs.
