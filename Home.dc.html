Working Principle

How Does an Electrostatic Precipitator Work?

An ESP removes particulate electrically, not mechanically. The sequence below follows one pass of process gas through a precipitator: dust-laden gas entering the inlet, the discharge electrodes energised by the TR set, corona charging of the dust, migration of the charged particles across the gas lane, collection on the plates, the rapping cycle releasing the dust layer, dust falling into the hoppers, and cleaned gas leaving for the stack.

Field Cross-Section

Looking along the gas flow

Collecting electrodes are vertical plates hung in parallel rows, typically 300–400 mm apart. A vertical discharge electrode hangs on the centreline of every gas lane, midway between two plates.

Corona discharge forms along the electrode and charges the dust. The charged particles then migrate sideways onto the plate faces on either side. Gas travels into the page.

Collection efficiency depends on field strength, electrode condition, gas distribution and a rapping cycle that actually releases the dust layer. Worn discharge electrodes, distorted collecting plates, failed rappers and obsolete controls all show up the same way — as a rising stack reading. Aeon Tech Solutions works on all four.
